# YouTube SEO Framework (2026)

How YouTube actually ranks and distributes video now, and how to write for it. Grounded in
2026 practice: **~70% of watch time comes from recommendations, not search**, and the
algorithm behaves like a *prediction engine* — it guesses what a viewer will watch next and
for how long. Optimize for **satisfaction and session time**, not keyword density.

## The two tracks (optimize for both)

| Track | What it rewards | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Search** | Relevance to a query | Keyword in title, first line of description, spoken in first 30s, transcript, chapters, tags |
| **Recommendation** (Browse/Suggested) | Predicted watch time, CTR, session time | Strong packaging (title+thumbnail), retention, "watch next" loops, playlists/series |

A keyword-perfect video that people watch for 30 seconds ranks **below** a lighter-optimized
video with 8-minute average watch time. Retention wins.

## Ranking signals, in priority order
1. **Audience retention** (Average % viewed & Average view duration) — the master metric.
2. **Click-through rate (CTR)** on impressions — packaging (title + thumbnail).
3. **Session time** — did this video keep the viewer *on YouTube* afterward?
4. **Engagement** — likes, comments, shares, subscribes-from-video.
5. **Metadata relevance** — title, description, tags, chapters, transcript.
6. **Freshness & consistency** — regular cadence trains the algorithm.

## Writing for retention (the script's job)
- **Hook (0–15s):** state the payoff + the stakes + a curiosity gap. No logo intro, no "hey
  guys welcome back." Earn the next 15 seconds immediately.
- **Value promise:** tell them exactly what they'll get and why it's worth staying.
- **Open loops:** tease a later payoff ("the third tool is the one that saved me 10 hours —
  I'll show you at the end").
- **Pattern interrupts** every 30–45s: change of pace, visual, example, question, B-roll.
- **Re-hooks** before predictable drop-off points (after intro, at mid-point).
- **Deliver density:** cut throat-clearing. Every line earns its place.
- **End screen hand-off:** point to the next episode → protects session time & drives bingeing.

## Packaging (drives CTR — half the battle)
- **Title:** front-load the primary keyword; ≤60 chars so it's not truncated; pair clarity
  with curiosity or a number/outcome. Avoid clickbait the video can't pay off (kills retention).
- **Thumbnail:** one idea, ≤3 words of text, high contrast, a face/emotion or a clear object,
  readable at phone size. Title and thumbnail should say *different* things (don't repeat).
- **A/B the packaging**, not the content — test up to 3 thumbnails via YouTube's tool.

## Metadata checklist (per video)
- [ ] Primary keyword in first 60 chars of title
- [ ] Keyword in first 1–2 lines of description (above the fold)
- [ ] 150–300 word description with keyword + related terms + timestamps + links
- [ ] **Chapters** (timestamps) — improves search, key-moments, and GEO extractability
- [ ] 8–12 relevant tags (primary, variants, entities) — minor signal, still do it
- [ ] Custom thumbnail + end screen + pinned comment with the "watch next" link
- [ ] Added to the **series playlist** (binge → session time)

## Series & session-time strategy
- A coherent **series/playlist** compounds: each video feeds the next → longer sessions →
  the algorithm promotes the whole cluster.
- Consistent packaging across the series builds a recognizable "shelf."
- Publish on a predictable cadence; sequence episodes so early wins pull viewers deeper.
