### [0:00–0:12] COLD-OPEN HOOK
**VO:** "You think you're doing three things at once. You're not. You're doing one thing badly, then
another thing badly, then switching back and wondering why nothing feels finished. Multitasking isn't

**[ON-SCREEN:** WHY MULTITASKING MAKES YOU WORSE]** · [a brain juggling three balls, dropping all of them]

### [0:12–0:35] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here's the short version. Your brain can't run two focused tasks at the same time. What feels
like multitasking is really fast switching — flicking your attention from one thing to the next. And
every switch has a cost: a slice of your focus stays stuck on the thing you just left. Do that
hundreds of times a day and you're slower, sloppier, and more drained than if you'd done one thing at
a time. That's the new rule: stop multitasking. Single-task — because switching is the tax."
**[ON-SCREEN:** No such thing as multitasking → it's fast switching → each switch costs focus + time]**

### [0:35–0:52] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: what's really happening in your brain when you 'multitask,' why it
makes you slower and more tired at the same time, and the one rule that gets your focus back. Stay to
the end — because the most expensive switch you make isn't between two big tasks. It's a tiny one you
do every few minutes without even noticing."

### [0:52–1:55] BODY 1 — There is no such thing as multitasking
**VO:** "Start with the myth itself. You cannot consciously focus on two things at once. Your brain
has one spotlight of attention, and it can only point at one place. So when you 'multitask,' you're
not splitting your focus — you're whipping that spotlight back and forth, fast enough to fool yourself
into thinking it's in two places."
**VO:** "Picture writing an email while on a call. You're not doing both. You catch a sentence of the
call, lose the email's thread, snap back to the email, miss what was just said. You feel busy. But
you're really just a spotlight bouncing off two walls, lighting up neither."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** single spotlight snapping frantically between two doors, both left dark ]**
**VO:** "So the skill you're proud of isn't parallel processing. It's rapid abandonment."
> You're not doing two things at once. You're doing neither, in quick succession.

### [1:55–3:00] BODY 2 — The switching tax
**VO:** "Now here's why it makes you worse, not just busier. Every time you switch tasks, a piece of
your attention stays stuck on the last one. Psychologists have a name for that leftover: attention
residue. So you arrive at the new task already carrying baggage from the old one — and you never get
your full mind on either. Research on task-switching is blunt about the cost: you're slower and you
make more mistakes."
**VO:** "And it's not free energetically. All that reloading — where was I, what was I doing — burns
mental fuel. That's why an afternoon of 'juggling' leaves you fried but with nothing actually
finished. You didn't do more. You paid the switching tax, over and over, all day."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Each switch leaves 'attention residue' → slower + more errors + more tired → less done]**
> Multitasking doesn't multiply your output. It multiplies your switching bill.

### [3:00–3:35] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "So if switching is the tax, the fix isn't to switch faster or get better at juggling. It's to
switch less. And there's one habit hiding in your day that's charging you this tax dozens of times an
hour — and it's not the big stuff. It's smaller, and it's in your pocket."

### [3:35–4:30] BODY 3 — The new rule + The One-Tab Rule  (open-loop payoff)
**VO:** "Here's the new rule: single-task, and treat every switch as a cost you pay on purpose. The
simplest way to enforce that is what I'll call **the One-Tab Rule.** One task, one tab, one window —
everything else closed, not minimized. Because a tab you can see is a switch waiting to happen."
**VO:** "And that tiny, expensive switch I promised you? It's the glance. Checking your phone
'just for a second' mid-task. That second isn't the cost — the cost is the residue it leaves and the
climb back to focus, which can take minutes. Do it every few minutes and you never actually reach deep
focus at all. The One-Tab Rule closes those doors before your spotlight can jump. One thing, finished,
beats five things, half-done."
**[ON-SCREEN:** One task, one tab — close everything else (a glance is a switch)]**
> The phone glance is cheap. The way back to focus is not.

### [4:30–5:00] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — there's no such thing as multitasking. There's only switching, and switching is a tax on
your speed, your accuracy, and your energy. The fix isn't to juggle better. It's to put the balls down
and do one thing at a time. That's rule seven: single-task, and stop paying the switching tax. Your
move today: pick your next task and give it one tab, one window, nothing else. Next, we get practical —
because knowing all this still doesn't make you start. Episode 8 shows you how to actually stop
procrastinating, without relying on discipline. Subscribe so you don't miss it. New rules only."
