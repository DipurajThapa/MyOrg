**[ON-SCREEN:** WHY STARTING FEELS SO HARD]** · [a tiny figure frozen at the foot of an enormous step]

### [0:12–0:58] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here's why starting feels so hard. It isn't a willpower problem — it's a threat response.
When you picture a task you're unsure about, a small part of your brain called the amygdala — your
built-in alarm — flags it as danger. It can't tell the difference between a difficult report and a
physical threat. So it floods you with one urge: escape. And the easiest escape is doing anything
else. The old rule says push through the resistance. The new rule: shrink the task below the fear, so
the alarm never fires. Quick note — this is mindset, not medical advice. If the dread is constant or
overwhelming, that's not a diagnosis; see a professional."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Starting feels hard because your brain flags the task as a threat — not because you're lazy]**

### [0:58–1:15] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: why the dread is always worse than the task, why 'just push through'
makes it worse, and the one move that switches the alarm off. Stay to the end — because there's a line
that decides whether you start or stall, and almost nobody finds it on purpose."

### [1:15–2:18] BODY 1 — The dread is the problem, not the task
**VO:** "Start with the part that sounds impossible but is true: the task is almost never as bad as
the dread. Psychologists call it anticipatory anxiety — the fear before a thing is usually worse than
the thing. Your brain runs a horror-movie preview of how hard, how boring, how uncomfortable it'll be.
And you don't avoid the task. You avoid the preview."
**VO:** "Think of the email you dreaded for a whole day. Sending it took ninety seconds. The dread
took eight hours. That gap — between how it felt in your head and how it actually went — that's the
whole trap."
**[ON-SCREEN:** You're not avoiding the task. You're avoiding how you imagine it'll feel.]**
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** a huge monster-shadow on the wall → a tiny object when the light flicks on]**
> You're not avoiding the work. You're avoiding the story you told yourself about it.

### [2:18–3:20] BODY 2 — Why "push through" backfires
**VO:** "So why doesn't forcing it work? Because you can't out-muscle an alarm. The louder you tell
yourself 'this is important, do it NOW,' the bigger the task feels — and the bigger it feels, the
harder the amygdala rings. Pressure doesn't shrink the fear. It feeds it."
**VO:** "And there's a pattern to what sets it off: the vaguer and bigger the task, the louder the
alarm. 'Write the report' is huge and shapeless — pure threat. 'Open the document' is nothing. Same
project. Completely different alarm."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Big + vague = louder alarm. Small + specific = silence.]**
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** a volume knob cranking up, then snapping to zero]**
> You can't force your way past an alarm. You have to make it stop ringing.

### [3:20–3:50] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "So if pushing harder only makes it louder, what actually turns the alarm off? One move — and
it's far smaller than you think. Here's the new rule."

### [3:50–4:40] BODY 3 — Shrink below the Threat Threshold  (open-loop payoff)
**VO:** "The new rule is this: shrink the task below the fear. There's a size at which your brain stops
treating a task as dangerous — call it the Threat Threshold. Above it, the alarm fires and you stall.
Below it, the task is too small to be worth an alarm, so you just… start. Your only job is to find the
version of the first step that lands under that line."
**VO:** "Not 'write the report.' Not even 'write the intro.' 'Open the doc and type one ugly
sentence.' Not 'go to the gym.' 'Put your shoes on.' It feels almost stupidly small — that's the
point. Stupidly small is invisible to your alarm. And once you've started, the preview turns out to be
wrong, the dread drains, and continuing is easy. Starting was never the whole battle. It was the only
battle."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Shrink the first step until it's too small to be scary.]**
> Make the first step so small your brain doesn't bother sounding the alarm.

### [4:40–5:05] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — starting feels hard because your brain flags the unknown as a threat, and the dread is
almost always worse than the task itself. Pushing through just cranks the alarm. The fix is the new
rule: shrink the task below the fear, until the first step slips under your Threat Threshold. Your move
today: take the thing you've been avoiding, and name the smallest first step that feels almost too easy
to count. Then do only that. Next, we take on the belief that sabotages you more than any task ever
could — that you need to feel motivated first. Episode 6 shows you why motivation was never the engine,
