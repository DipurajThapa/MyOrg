### [0:12–0:35] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here's the short version. Motivation is a feeling, and feelings are unreliable — they show up
late, leave early, and disappear the second a task gets boring. That's why waiting for motivation
keeps failing you. The people who get things done aren't more motivated than you. They've built
systems that remove the decision, so the work starts whether they feel like it or not. Motivation
asks, 'do I want to?' A system never asks. That's the new rule: don't wait for motivation — remove
the decision."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Motivation = a feeling (unreliable) → systems remove the decision → work starts anyway]**

### [0:35–0:52] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: why motivation is built to fail you, why 'just be more disciplined'

willpower you have."

### [0:52–1:55] BODY 1 — Motivation is a feeling, and feelings lie
**VO:** "First, let's be honest about what motivation actually is. It's a mood. A chemical weather
system. Some mornings you wake up ready to move mountains; most mornings you don't. Nothing changed
about your goals overnight — only your feelings did. So when you tie your work to a feeling, you've
handed your future to the least reliable part of you."
**VO:** "Think about the gym. You don't struggle because you forgot you want to be healthy. You
struggle because at 6 a.m., the *feeling* isn't there. It rarely is. And if the plan only works on
the days you feel like it, it was never a plan."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** weather forecast graphic — "MOTIVATION: scattered, 20% chance" ]**
**VO:** "Waiting to feel ready isn't patience. It's a stall tactic your brain uses to avoid discomfort."
> Motivation is the mood you're hoping shows up. A system doesn't need it to.

### [1:55–3:00] BODY 2 — Discipline isn't the answer either
**VO:** "Now, the usual comeback is: 'okay, so just be more disciplined.' But that's the same trap
wearing a tougher jacket. Discipline is willpower, and willpower is a limited battery. Every choice
you make all day — what to eat, what to answer, what to ignore — draws that battery down. Psychologists
call the result decision fatigue. By evening, there's nothing left to 'push through' with."
**VO:** "So if your entire strategy is out-muscling yourself, you'll win some days and lose most. Not
because you're weak — because you're running on a resource that empties. The strongest people you know
aren't grinding harder. They've arranged their life so they barely have to."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Willpower drains all day → by evening the tank is empty → discipline alone can't last]**
> You can't out-discipline a battery that's designed to run down.

### [3:00–3:35] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "So if motivation is unreliable and discipline runs out — what's left? Something almost no one
talks about, because it isn't inspiring and it doesn't sell posters. It's boring. It's mechanical.
And it's the only thing that actually works: you take the decision out of the moment entirely."

### [3:35–4:30] BODY 3 — The new rule + The One-Decision System  (open-loop payoff)
**VO:** "Here's the new rule: don't rely on motivation or willpower — build a system that removes the
decision. And the cleanest version of that is what I'll call **the One-Decision System.** You decide
once, on paper, in a calm moment: *when* the work happens, *where*, and *what* the very first move is.
Then you never decide again. Same time, same place, same tiny first step. The choice is already made,
so there's nothing left for motivation to veto."
**VO:** "Remember that decision I said you make dozens of times a day? It's the question 'should I
start now, or in a minute?' Every time you leave that open, your tired brain votes 'later.' The
One-Decision System closes the question before the day even starts. You don't argue with yourself —
because there's no argument left to have."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Decide ONCE: when + where + first move → then stop deciding]**
> Motivation gets a vote every day. A system takes the vote away.

### [4:30–5:00] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — motivation is a feeling, and feelings are the worst thing to build on. Discipline helps,
but it drains by dinner. The move that lasts is to remove the decision: decide once, then let the
system carry you on the days you don't feel a thing. That's rule six — systems win. Your job today:
pick one task, and lock its when, where, and first step. One decision, made once. Next, we take on the
