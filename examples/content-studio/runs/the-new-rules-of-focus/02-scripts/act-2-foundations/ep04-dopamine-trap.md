### [0:12–0:55] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here's what dopamine actually has to do with focus. Dopamine isn't the pleasure chemical —
it's the wanting chemical. It doesn't reward you for finishing something. It pushes you to chase the
next thing. Every cheap, fast hit — a notification, a snack, a new video — spikes it, and your brain
quietly raises its baseline for what feels worth doing. So slow, real work starts to feel flat. The
old rule says chase the reward. The new rule flips it: lower the baseline, and protect your dopamine
for what actually matters."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Dopamine = wanting, not liking → cheap hits raise your baseline → real work feels flat]**

### [0:55–1:12] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: why chasing rewards leaves you emptier, the hidden bill you're
paying every time you reach for your phone, and the one shift that makes hard work feel worth doing
again. Stay to the end — because there's a simple order-of-operations most people get backwards, and
it quietly drains every reward before they've earned it."

### [1:12–2:15] BODY 1 — Wanting isn't liking
**VO:** "Start with the thing nobody tells you about dopamine. It doesn't make you happy. It makes you
want. Scientists separate two systems in the brain: wanting and liking. Dopamine runs wanting — the
itch, the pull, the seeking. That's why you can scroll for an hour and feel worse at the end than when
you started. You weren't chasing enjoyment. You were chasing the chase."
**VO:** "Think of refreshing your inbox when you're bored. There's nothing good in there. You know
that. You refresh anyway — because the wanting fires before the liking ever gets a vote."
**[ON-SCREEN:** You're not chasing enjoyment. You're chasing the wanting.]**
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** a dog sprinting after a car it can never catch]**
> Dopamine doesn't pay you for finishing. It pays you for chasing.

### [2:15–3:20] BODY 2 — The Dopamine Debt
**VO:** "Now here's the part that costs you. Every cheap hit works like borrowing. The spike feels
good for a second, but your brain adjusts — it resets the baseline higher, so next time it takes more
just to feel normal. I call it the Dopamine Debt. You're not buying pleasure. You're taking a loan
against tomorrow's focus, and the interest is that ordinary effort now feels boring."
**VO:** "There's a name for the other half of this: temporal discounting. Your brain massively
overvalues rewards it can have right now, and discounts rewards that pay off later. A video pays out
in one second. A finished project pays out in a week. To your reward system, that's not a fair fight."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Every quick hit is borrowed — the bill is that effort feels flat]**
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** a card swipe → an account balance dropping to zero]**
> Every quick hit is a loan against tomorrow's focus — paid back with interest.

### [3:20–3:50] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "So if your brain is wired to chase now and discount later, how do you win? Not with more
willpower — you'll lose that fight every time. You win by changing one thing: the order you take your
rewards in. Here's the new rule."

### [3:50–4:40] BODY 3 — Protect the baseline  (open-loop payoff)
**VO:** "The new rule is this: stop chasing rewards, and start earning them. Lower your baseline so
real work stops feeling flat — and put the reward *after* the effort, not before. Most people do it
backwards. They scroll, snack, and stack three hits at once before they've done anything — so they
spend their best dopamine on nothing, and arrive at the actual work already numb."
**VO:** "Two moves. First: earn before you spend — do fifteen minutes of the real thing before you
touch the hit. Second: stop stacking — don't eat and scroll and watch all at once. One input at a
time. You're not banning pleasure. You're refusing to spend it before you've earned it, so it means
something again."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Earn the reward. Don't borrow it.]**
> Do the work first — then the reward feels like a reward again.

### [4:40–5:05] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — dopamine isn't there to make you happy. It's there to make you chase. Chase cheap hits
all day and you run up a debt that makes real work feel flat. The fix isn't force. It's the new rule:
lower the baseline, protect your dopamine, and earn your rewards instead of borrowing them. Your move
today: pick one hit you reach for on autopilot — the scroll, the snack, the video — and do fifteen
minutes of real work before you take it. Next, we go after something even more frustrating: why
starting a task can feel physically impossible, even when you genuinely want to do it. Episode 5 shows
you what's really happening. Subscribe so you don't miss it. New rules only."
