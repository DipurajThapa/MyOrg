**VO:** "For fourteen episodes we retired one focus myth after another. Willpower. Laziness. Broken
attention. Every one of them was wrong. Today we put the replacements in your hands — ten new rules

### [0:13–0:45] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here are the new rules of focus in 2026, short version. One: you don't need more willpower,
you need less stimulation. Two: your attention isn't broken, it's hijacked by design. Three:
procrastination is a feeling to face, not a flaw to fix. Four: protect your dopamine baseline instead
of chasing hits. Five: shrink the task below the fear. Six: build systems, not motivation. Seven:
single-task — switching is the tax. Eight: go deep, because depth is the edge. Nine: design your
environment, not your self-control. Ten: discipline is kindness. That's the playbook. Now let's make
it usable."
**[ON-SCREEN:** THE 10 NEW RULES — numbered list, 1–10]**

### [0:45–1:02] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes I'll walk all ten rules with the one line you actually need from
each, then give you the single move that installs them — and the identity shift underneath it all.
Stay to the end — because the mistake most people make here is trying to do all ten at once, and
there's a reason that guarantees you quit by Thursday."

### [1:02–2:20] BODY 1 — Rules 1 through 5: quiet the brain, lower the bar to start
**VO:** "Rules one through five are about your brain and the starting line. Rule one, from where this
all began: you can't out-willpower a brain trained to chase quick hits — so subtract the stimulation,
don't add force. Rule two: your short attention span isn't a character flaw, it's engineered against
you, so drop the shame. Rule three: procrastination isn't laziness, it's an emotion — usually fear —
so name the feeling you're avoiding instead of calling yourself lazy. Rule four: every quick hit
raises your baseline and makes real work feel flat, so protect that baseline like it's the asset it
is. Rule five: starting feels hard because your brain flags the task as a threat — so shrink it until
starting is laughably easy."
**[ON-SCREEN:** 1 Subtract stimulation · 2 It's hijacked, not broken · 3 Name the feeling · 4 Protect
the baseline · 5 Shrink below the fear]**
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** a mountain labeled "the task" shrinking to a single step someone just walks over]**
> You don't win focus by forcing it. You win by removing what's stealing it.

### [2:20–3:35] BODY 2 — Rules 6 through 10: build the conditions, then be kind
**VO:** "Rules six through ten are about the conditions around you. Rule six: motivation is weather —
it comes and goes — so build a system that removes the decision, because systems beat motivation
every time. Rule seven: multitasking isn't doing more, it's switching, and switching is a tax on
every task — so single-task. Rule eight: shallow busywork is the default now, which makes real depth
rare and valuable — so go deep, and engineer the conditions instead of waiting for inspiration. Rule
nine: don't spend willpower fighting your phone, design the friction and change your environment, and
let yourself be bored so your attention can recover. And rule ten, the one that holds the other nine
together: discipline is kindness — you keep going by restarting gently, not by beating yourself up."
**[ON-SCREEN:** 6 Systems > motivation · 7 Single-task · 8 Go deep · 9 Design the friction · 10
Discipline is kindness]**
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** ten separate gears clicking into one turning machine]**
> The old rules asked you to be stronger. The new rules ask you to be smarter about the setup.

### [3:35–4:05] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "Ten rules. And right now your instinct is to overhaul your whole life on Monday and run all
ten. Don't. That's the exact move that fails — too much change at once is a system your brain rejects.
Here's what actually installs this."

### [4:05–5:05] BODY 3 — The One-Rule Week + the identity shift  (open-loop payoff)
**VO:** "Here's your first move, and I call it the One-Rule Week. Pick a single rule from the ten —
just one — and run only that for seven days. If you don't know which, start where the series started:
protect the first thirty minutes of your morning from the feed. One rule, one week. When it feels
normal, add the next. That's it. This is rule thirteen in action — shrink it, anchor it, and let it
stick before you stack more.
Now the part underneath all of it. Every rule here quietly changes one thing: who you think you are.
You are not a distracted person trying to focus. You're a person who is learning to practice depth —
and that identity is built one kept promise at a time. Focus was never a trait you were born with or
without. It's a set of conditions you design, and a person you slowly become."
**[ON-SCREEN:** THE ONE-RULE WEEK — pick 1 rule · run 7 days · then stack. Identity: "I practice depth."]**
> You don't find your focus. You build the conditions, and you become the kind of person who has it.
