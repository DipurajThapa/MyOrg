### [0:00–0:12] COLD-OPEN HOOK
**VO:** "You have one task that matters. It's been on your list for three days. And somehow you've
tidied your desk, cleared your inbox, and made a second coffee — anything but that one thing. That's
not laziness. And 'get disciplined' won't fix it."
**[ON-SCREEN:** HOW TO ACTUALLY STOP PROCRASTINATING]** · [a hand hovering over one task, sliding to

### [0:12–0:38] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here's the short version. You stop procrastinating by shrinking the task until it stops
feeling threatening. Procrastination isn't a willpower problem — it's your brain avoiding a bad
feeling attached to the task: fear, boredom, or overwhelm. Discipline doesn't remove the feeling, so
you keep flinching away. The fix is to make the first step so small it slips under the fear. That's
the new rule: don't get more disciplined — make starting laughably easy."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Procrastination = avoiding a feeling → shrink the first step → the flinch disappears]**

### [0:38–0:54] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: why 'just do it' backfires, exactly how small your first step needs
to be, and a line you can use to disarm your own resistance. Stay to the end — because there's a
thirty-second window right after you start that decides whether you keep going, and almost nobody
uses it."

### [0:54–1:58] BODY 1 — It's a feeling, not a flaw
**VO:** "First, kill the discipline myth. When you avoid a task, you're not avoiding the work — you're
avoiding how the work makes you feel. A blank document feels like the fear of doing it badly. A big
project feels like overwhelm. Your brain treats that feeling as a small threat, and it does what
brains do with threats: it moves you toward relief. Checking your phone *is* the relief."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** blank page → brain flags 'threat' → hand grabs phone]**
**VO:** "So piling on more discipline is like flooring the gas with the parking brake on. You're
fighting force with force, and the feeling is still there."
> You're not avoiding the task. You're avoiding the feeling attached to it.

### [1:58–3:04] BODY 2 — Shrink it below the Cringe Line
**VO:** "So here's the move. Make the first step so small it feels almost silly. Not 'write the
report' — write one sentence. Not 'go to the gym' — put on your shoes. Not 'clean the kitchen' — clear
one plate. There's a point where the step gets so tiny that your resistance can't justify showing up.
I call that the Cringe Line — the size where starting feels almost embarrassingly easy. Your job is
to go *below* it. If a two-minute version still feels heavy, shrink it again."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Find the Cringe Line — shrink the first step until it feels almost too small]**
**VO:** "This isn't a trick to do less. It's a trick to start at all — because the feeling can't
block a step it doesn't take seriously."
> The task you're dreading isn't too hard. Your first step is too big.

### [3:04–3:38] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "But shrinking the start is only half of it. Getting *in* the door is easy to teach. Staying
in the room is where people lose it — they take the tiny step, feel the relief, and drift right back
to the feed. So there's one thing you do the moment after you start that changes everything."

### [3:38–4:32] BODY 3 — Use the momentum window  (open-loop payoff)
**VO:** "Here it is. Once you've started, you get a short window where the resistance is gone — you're
already moving, and moving is easier than starting. Don't waste it deciding what's next. Just keep the
same tiny action going: one more sentence, one more plate. And when you stop, mark the win — even a
quiet 'nice.' It sounds soft, but you're teaching your brain that starting felt good, so next time it
flinches less. Shrink it, anchor it to something you already do, celebrate it. That's how a start
becomes a habit."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Right after you start: keep the tiny action going → then mark the win]**
> Starting is the hard part. So make starting the only part.

### [4:32–5:02] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — you procrastinate because your brain is dodging a feeling, and no amount of discipline
deletes that feeling. The new rule: don't force it, shrink it. Drop your first step below the Cringe
Line, ride the momentum window, and mark the win. Your move today: take the task you've been avoiding
and name a first step so small it's almost funny. Then do only that. Next, we flip from starting to
depth — because in a world this distracted, the ability to focus hard has quietly become a superpower.
Episode 9 shows you what deep work really is. Subscribe so you don't miss it. New rules only."
