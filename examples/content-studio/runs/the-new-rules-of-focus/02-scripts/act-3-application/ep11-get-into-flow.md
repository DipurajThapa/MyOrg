**OWNS THE QUESTION:** How do I get into flow?

"Engineer the conditions — flow is built, not summoned."

### [0:00–0:12] COLD-OPEN HOOK
**VO:** "There's a state where work stops feeling like work — where time disappears and the task almost
does itself. You've felt it. And you've probably been told it only shows up when you're inspired.
That's the lie. Flow isn't luck. It's a set of conditions you can build on purpose."
**[ON-SCREEN:** FIND FLOW]** · [a blurred desk snapping into sharp focus]

### [0:12–0:35] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here's the short version. You don't get into flow by waiting to feel motivated — you get there
by setting up the conditions flow needs, then giving your brain a few uninterrupted minutes to climb
in. The conditions are simple: one clear goal, a task that's a little harder than easy, and zero
interruptions. Miss any one and flow can't start. Hit all three and it arrives almost on its own.
That's the new rule: don't wait for inspiration. Engineer the conditions."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Flow needs → one clear goal · challenge just above skill · zero interruptions]**

### [0:35–0:52] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: why waiting for inspiration keeps you stuck, the three conditions
every flow state needs, and the short on-ramp that gets you there on command. Stay to the end —
because the reason you keep falling out of flow is probably one small thing you do every few minutes
without even noticing."

### [0:52–1:55] BODY 1 — Inspiration is a terrible strategy
**VO:** "Start with the advice that quietly ruins your best work: wait until you feel inspired. When
you do that, you hand control of your focus to a feeling — and feelings don't keep a schedule. So you
sit down, don't feel it, and call yourself unproductive. But inspiration isn't what starts flow. It's
usually the reward for starting anyway. The feeling follows the work; it never leads it."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** an 'inspiration' lightning bolt sputtering out]**
**VO:** "Waiting to feel ready is just procrastination in a nicer outfit. The people who reach flow
often aren't more inspired than you. They just stopped waiting for permission."
> Inspiration is what you feel after you start — not permission to begin.

### [1:55–3:00] BODY 2 — The three conditions
**VO:** "So if not inspiration — then what actually switches flow on? It has an entry code, and it's
three digits. One: a clear goal. Your brain needs to know exactly what 'done' looks like, or it won't
fully commit. Two: the right difficulty. Too easy and you drift off; too hard and you panic — flow
lives in the narrow band just past comfortable. Three: no interruptions. Flow builds like heat, and
every notification cracks a window and lets it out. Get those three right and you're not forcing
focus. You're just meeting its conditions."
**[ON-SCREEN:** The entry code — clear goal · challenge just above skill · no interruptions]**
> You don't summon flow. You qualify for it.

### [3:00–3:35] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "But here's the part almost everyone skips. Even with the conditions perfect, you can't flip
flow on like a switch. It has a warm-up — a stretch of slightly boring, slightly awkward minutes
before the good part kicks in. Most people quit right there, reach for their phone, and never make it
in. So here's how to get through it."

### [3:35–4:30] BODY 3 — The Flow Runway  (open-loop payoff)
**VO:** "Flow needs a runway — a few quiet minutes of just doing the task, badly if you have to, before
it lifts off. So build one. Pick one task. Write the single goal on paper. Put your phone in another
room. Then set a timer and promise yourself just one thing: you stay on the task, no switching, until
the timer runs out — even when it feels slow at first. Those opening minutes are supposed to feel like
nothing's happening. That's the runway, not a wall. Stay on it, and somewhere in there the work grabs
the controls and takes over."
**[ON-SCREEN:** The Flow Runway — one task · one goal on paper · phone away · timer, no switching]**
**VO:** "You're not chasing a mood anymore. You're taxiing down a runway you built, on purpose, every
single time."
> Flow doesn't strike. It takes off — but only if you give it a runway.

### [4:30–5:00] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — you don't get into flow by waiting to feel inspired. You get there by building the
conditions and riding out the runway. That's the new rule: engineer the conditions, and flow shows up
on schedule. Your move today: pick one task, write one clear goal, put the phone in another room, and
protect the first ten minutes without switching. Next, we flip the whole thing around — why the
boredom you keep escaping is the exact thing rebuilding your focus, and how to train for it. Episode
