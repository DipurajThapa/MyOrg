### [0:00–0:12] COLD-OPEN HOOK
**VO:** "Everyone around you is busy. Slack's lit up, twelve tabs open, calendar packed wall to wall.
And yet, by Friday, almost nobody can point to one thing that actually moved. Busy has become the
water we swim in. But busy isn't the flex anymore. Depth is."
**[ON-SCREEN:** DEEP WORK — FOCUS IS NOW A SUPERPOWER]** · [a full calendar dissolving into one
glowing block of focus]

### [0:12–0:40] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here's the short version. Deep work is focused, distraction-free effort on a single, hard
task — the opposite of shallow, reactive busywork like email and pings. The term comes from computer
scientist Cal Newport. His argument: as more of the easy work gets automated, and almost everyone else
stays distracted, the ability to concentrate deeply is becoming rare — and rare things are valuable.
That's the new rule: don't stay busy — go deep, because depth is now the edge."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Deep work = one hard task, zero distractions (Cal Newport) → rare skill → real edge]**

### [0:40–0:56] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: why busy work feels productive but quietly isn't, why depth keeps
getting rarer — and therefore more valuable — and how to protect one real block of it. Stay to the
end, because there's a reason most people can't focus deeply for long yet — and a simple way that
capacity grows."

### [0:56–2:00] BODY 1 — Busy is a decoy
**VO:** "First, see the trap. Shallow work is anything easy, reactive, and low-value: clearing your
inbox, hopping on the fifth call, reacting to whatever's loudest. It feels productive because it's
motion, and motion looks like progress. But you can spend a whole day in shallow work and create
nothing that lasts. Deep work is the opposite — it's the writing, the building, the thinking that
actually compounds. And it's the first thing distraction steals."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** inbox-zero confetti → then an empty 'what did I make?' box]**
**VO:** "So the question isn't 'was I busy today.' Everyone's busy. The question is 'did I go deep on
anything that matters.'"
> Busy is the easiest way to look productive while producing nothing.

### [2:00–3:06] BODY 2 — Depth is a moat
**VO:** "Now here's why this is a superpower and not just a nice habit. Value follows scarcity. When
almost everyone is distracted, the few who can sit with one hard problem for ninety uninterrupted
minutes can do things the distracted simply can't — and can't be easily copied. I think of it as a
Focus Moat: a gap between you and everyone else that widens every time they reach for their phone and
you don't. They can't cross it, because crossing it means putting the phone down — and they won't.
The moat isn't talent. It's attention nobody else is willing to spend."
**[ON-SCREEN:** The Focus Moat — depth is an edge others can't copy while they stay distracted]**
**VO:** "So depth isn't just how you get more done. It's how you become hard to replace."
> When focus is rare, the ability to focus is a competitive advantage.

### [3:06–3:40] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "By now you might be thinking: great, but I can't focus for ninety minutes — I lose it after
fifteen. Good. That's not a disqualification. That's the starting line. Because deep work isn't a
switch you flip. It's a muscle — and here's how you build it without hating the process."

### [3:40–4:34] BODY 3 — Build the capacity  (open-loop payoff)
**VO:** "Here's the payoff. Deep focus is a capacity you grow, not a trait you're born with. So don't
book a three-hour session and fail. Start with one protected block — phone in another room, one task,
a timer. Twenty-five minutes if ninety feels impossible. Then, over weeks, stretch it. Same idea as
everything on this channel: shrink it below the resistance, anchor it to a fixed time of day, and let
it grow. The point isn't a heroic marathon on day one. It's proving to your brain that undistracted
focus is a place it can go — and then going there a little longer each week."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Protect one block — phone away, one task, a timer → stretch it week by week]**
> You don't find time for deep work. You defend it.

### [4:34–5:04] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — deep work is undistracted effort on one hard thing, and in a distracted world that
skill has quietly become rare, which makes it valuable. The new rule: stop measuring yourself by busy,
and start building depth. Protect one block, guard it like it matters, and stretch it over time — that's
your Focus Moat. Your move today: block thirty minutes, phone in another room, one task, nothing else.
Next, we go after the thing standing between you and every block of focus you just promised yourself —
your phone — and we beat it without relying on willpower. Episode 10. Subscribe so you don't miss it.
New rules only."
