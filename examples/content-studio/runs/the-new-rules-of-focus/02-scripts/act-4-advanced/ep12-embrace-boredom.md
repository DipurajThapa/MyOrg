**OWNS THE QUESTION:** How do I fix a short attention span?
**KEYWORD:** short attention span · **OLD RULE:** "Fill every gap" → **NEW RULE:** "Train tolerance
for boredom."

---

### [0:00–0:12] COLD-OPEN HOOK
**VO:** "You're standing in line. Two seconds of nothing — and your phone is already in your hand.
You didn't decide to reach for it. That reflex, the one that fills every empty second, is the exact
thing shrinking your attention span. Today we reverse it."
**[ON-SCREEN:** FIX YOUR ATTENTION SPAN]** · [a hand reaching for a phone in an empty waiting room]

### [0:12–0:35] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here's the short version. A short attention span isn't damage — it's a low tolerance for
being under-stimulated. Every time you fill a boring gap with a scroll, you teach your brain that
boredom is an emergency to escape. Do that hundreds of times a day and your tolerance for
low-stimulation states — which is what focus actually is — collapses. The fix isn't a new app or more
discipline. It's letting yourself be bored on purpose. Boredom is how your brain rebuilds attention.
That's the new rule."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Fill every gap → boredom feels unbearable → your attention span shrinks]**

### [0:35–0:52] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: why boredom got a reputation it doesn't deserve, why filling every
gap quietly makes focus harder, and a simple daily practice that rebuilds your attention span from
the ground up. Stay to the end — because the most boring ninety seconds of your day might turn out to
be the most valuable."

### [0:52–1:55] BODY 1 — Boredom isn't the enemy
**VO:** "First, let's fix what you think boredom actually is. Boredom isn't emptiness. It's your
brain's signal that it's under-stimulated and ready to turn inward — to wander, connect ideas, plan,
reflect. That drifting, mind-wandering state is where a lot of real thinking and creativity happen.
But you almost never reach it anymore, because the instant boredom shows up, you feed it a screen."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** a blank elevator wall → a hand pulls out a phone]**
**VO:** "Think about the last time a good idea hit you — in the shower, on a walk, lying awake. Not
while scrolling. Those were boredom moments you happened not to interrupt."
> Boredom isn't a void to fill. It's a muscle you've stopped using.

### [1:55–3:00] BODY 2 — Why filling every gap backfires
**VO:** "Here's why 'always have something to watch' makes it worse. Every gap you fill trains one
reflex: discomfort, then escape. The line, the elevator, the ad break, the two seconds a page takes
to load — each one becomes a cue to reach for a hit. Your brain learns that it never has to sit with
under-stimulation. So when you finally need to focus — on your work, a book, a conversation — the
very first dull moment feels intolerable, and you bail."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Fill every gap → "boredom = emergency" → you quit anything slow]**
**VO:** "You didn't lose your attention span. You trained it to run."
> You don't have a short attention span. You have a low tolerance for being under-stimulated.

### [3:00–3:35] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "So if the problem is fleeing boredom, the fix is learning to stay in it — on purpose, in
small doses. And there's a stupidly simple way to practice it that turns your dead time into training
time. It costs nothing, and you already have a dozen chances to do it every day."

### [3:35–4:30] BODY 3 — The new rule + Boredom Reps  (open-loop payoff)
**VO:** "Here's the new rule: stop filling every gap, and let boredom do its job. You rebuild
attention the way you build a muscle — with reps. So here's the practice. Call them Boredom Reps.
Take the small dead moments you'd normally fill — waiting in line, the elevator, the kettle boiling —
and just... don't. Leave the phone in your pocket. Let your mind be bored. That's one rep."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Boredom Rep = one dead moment, phone stays in your pocket]**
**VO:** "You're not meditating. You're not achieving anything. You're teaching your brain that
under-stimulation isn't an emergency. Start with the ninety seconds it takes to make your coffee.
That's the whole exercise."
> Every time you resist the scroll, you're doing a rep. Attention is trained, not found.

### [4:30–5:00] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — a short attention span isn't broken wiring. It's a habit of escaping every boring
second, and every escape shrinks your tolerance a little more. The fix isn't more input. It's less.
That's the new rule: train your tolerance for boredom, and focus rebuilds itself. Your move today:
do three Boredom Reps — three dead moments where the phone stays in your pocket. Next, we tackle the
question everyone gets wrong — how to build a focus habit that actually sticks, instead of collapsing
by Wednesday. Episode 13 shows you the three moves that make it hold. Subscribe so you don't miss it.
New rules only."
**[ON-SCREEN:** End screen — Ep 13 "Build a Focus Habit That Sticks" + Subscribe]** · Pinned comment:
"When's the last time you let yourself be fully bored — no phone? Be honest."
