# Ep 2 Script — "Procrastination Isn't Laziness (It's Fear)"

**OWNS THE QUESTION:** Why do I procrastinate?
**KEYWORD:** why do I procrastinate · **OLD RULE:** "You're lazy" → **NEW RULE:** "You're avoiding a
feeling."

---

### [0:00–0:12] COLD-OPEN HOOK
**VO:** "You tell yourself you'll start in five minutes. Five minutes becomes an hour. The hour
becomes tomorrow. And every time, you call yourself lazy. You're not lazy. You're avoiding a feeling —
and today you learn to name it."
**[ON-SCREEN:** NOT LAZY]** · [a person frozen at a desk, a small storm cloud hovering over the task]

### [0:12–0:35] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here's the short version. You don't procrastinate because you're lazy or bad at managing
time. You procrastinate to escape an uncomfortable feeling the task brings up — fear of failing,
boredom, confusion, not knowing where to start. Putting it off gives you instant relief, so your
brain learns to do it again. Procrastination isn't a time problem. It's an emotion problem. That's
the second new rule of focus: you're not avoiding the work — you're avoiding a feeling."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Procrastination = escaping a feeling, not managing time]**

### [0:35–0:52] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: why 'just do it' never works, the hidden loop that keeps you stuck,
and the one question that breaks it. Stay to the end — because the feeling you're running from is
almost never the one you think it is."

### [0:52–1:55] BODY 1 — Fear wearing a disguise
**VO:** "First, kill the word lazy. Lazy people don't feel guilty. You do — constantly. That guilt is
the tell. Psychologists who study this have a sharper name for it: procrastination is emotion
regulation. When a task threatens to make you feel something unpleasant — you might fail, you might
look stupid, you don't even know where to begin — your brain does the fastest thing it can to feel
better right now. It avoids. You open the document, feel the dread, and check your phone instead. The
relief is immediate. That relief is the reward that trains you."
**[ON-SCREEN:** The task threatens a feeling → you avoid → instant relief]**
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** a hand reaching for a task, then flinching back like it touched a hot stove]**
> You're not avoiding the work. You're avoiding how the work makes you feel.

### [1:55–3:00] BODY 2 — The Avoidance Loop
**VO:** "Here's why it gets worse, not better. Call it the Avoidance Loop. A task makes you anxious.
You escape it. You feel relief — and then shame, for escaping. And shame makes the task feel heavier,
so next time the dread is bigger and the escape is faster. You're not stuck because you're weak.
You're stuck in a loop that pays you every single time you run. That's the trap: discipline can't
beat a system that hands out relief on demand."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Anxiety → escape → relief → shame → heavier task → repeat]**
> Every time you flee the task, your brain gets paid. That's why willpower loses.

### [3:00–3:35] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "So if the real problem is a feeling, the fix isn't a better to-do list or a stricter
schedule. It's learning to face the feeling long enough to start. And there's one move that does it
in about a minute — no motivation required."

### [3:35–4:35] BODY 3 — Name the fear  (open-loop payoff)
**VO:** "Here's the new rule: you're avoiding a feeling, so name it. The next time you feel the
resistance, stop and ask one question — what feeling am I trying to avoid right now? Then say the
honest answer out loud. 'I'm scared this won't be good enough.' 'I don't actually know how to start.'
'This is boring and I just want it over.' Naming the feeling does something strange — it shrinks it.
You can't fight a fog. You can face a sentence. Once the feeling has a name, the task stops being a
threat and goes back to being just a task."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Ask "What feeling am I avoiding?" — then say it out loud]**
> You can't fight a fog. You can face a sentence.
**VO:** "One honest note: if avoidance is constant and it's wrecking your work or your health, that
can point to something like anxiety or ADHD. This isn't a diagnosis — if it sounds like you, talk to
a professional."

### [4:35–4:50] SUBSCRIBE MOMENT
**VO:** "Next episode, we go after an even bigger lie — that your attention span is broken. It isn't.
It's being hijacked, on purpose, and Episode 3 shows you exactly who's doing it. Subscribe so you
don't miss it. New rules only."

### [4:50–5:10] CLOSE — PAYOFF + ACTION + HANDOFF
**VO:** "So — you don't procrastinate because you're lazy. You procrastinate to escape a feeling, and
every escape trains the next one. The fix isn't more discipline. It's naming the fear so it stops
running the show. That's rule two: you're not avoiding the work, you're avoiding a feeling. Your move
today: next time you stall, ask what feeling you're dodging — and say it out loud before you touch
anything else."
**[ON-SCREEN:** End screen — Ep 3 "Your Attention Span Isn't Broken" + Subscribe]** · Pinned comment:
"What's the one task you've avoided all week? Name the feeling under it."
