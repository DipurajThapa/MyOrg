### [0:00–0:12] COLD-OPEN HOOK
**VO:** "You sit down to work, and forty seconds later your hand reaches for your phone. You didn't
even decide to. If you can't focus like you used to, it's not a character flaw — your brain has been
trained to do exactly this. Today we untrain it."
**[ON-SCREEN:** WHY YOU CAN'T FOCUS]** · [a brain with a phone-shaped magnet pulling at it]

### [0:12–0:35] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here's the short version. You can't focus because your brain's reward system runs on
dopamine, and dopamine is wired to chase quick, unpredictable hits. Your apps are built on that
exact mechanism — the same one that makes slot machines addictive. So compared to an endless feed,
deep work feels boring, and your brain quietly pulls you back to the feed. The fix isn't more
willpower. It's less stimulation. That's the first new rule of focus."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Dopamine chases quick hits → deep work feels 'boring' → you drift]**

### [0:35–0:52] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: why your attention got hijacked, why fighting it with discipline
backfires, and the one change that rebuilds your focus. Stay to the end — because the thing you do
in the first five minutes of your day is quietly deciding how well you can focus for the rest of it."

### [0:52–1:55] BODY 1 — It's not you, it's the design
**VO:** "First, drop the guilt. Your short attention span isn't a personal failing. Your feeds are
engineered by teams of very smart people to capture exactly this. They use variable rewards — you
never know when the next funny, shocking, or satisfying thing will appear, so you keep scrolling to
find out. That uncertainty is the hook."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** slot-machine reels → phone notification]**
**VO:** "So you're not weak. You're up against a machine designed to beat your willpower. Blaming
yourself just adds shame on top of the problem."
> Your attention span isn't broken. It's being farmed.

### [1:55–3:00] BODY 2 — Why willpower loses
**VO:** "Here's why 'just focus harder' fails. Every time you get a quick hit — a like, a new video,
a message — you get a little dopamine. Do that all day and your brain resets its baseline high. Then
you open a document, or a book, and it feels flat, slow, almost painful — because compared to the
feed, it *is*. Neuroscientists have a word for that drop: it feels like withdrawal."
**[ON-SCREEN:** High-stimulation all day → normal work feels 'boring' → you escape]**
**VO:** "You can't out-discipline a brain in withdrawal for more than a few minutes. That's not
weakness. That's chemistry."
> You can't out-willpower a dopamine baseline you keep spiking.

### [3:00–3:35] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "So if grinding harder doesn't work, what does? It's the opposite of what productivity
culture tells you. You don't add more focus. You remove the thing stealing it. And there's one place
to start that changes everything else."

### [3:35–4:30] BODY 3 — The new rule + the one change  (open-loop payoff)
**VO:** "Here's the new rule: lower the stimulation, and focus comes back on its own. Your attention
isn't gone — it's just drowned out. And the highest-leverage place to start is those first five
minutes of your day. Most people wake up and immediately flood their brain with the feed. You've
spiked your dopamine before you've done anything — and every real task after that feels dull by
comparison."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Don't touch the feed for the first 30 minutes awake]**
**VO:** "So your one job after this video: don't touch the feed for the first thirty minutes you're
awake. Let your brain start the day at a normal baseline. It sounds almost too simple. It isn't —
you're refusing the first hit, so everything else stops feeling boring."
> Win the first 30 minutes and you win your attention for the day.

### [4:30–5:00] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — you can't focus because your brain's been trained to chase quick hits, and no amount of
willpower beats that chemistry. The fix isn't more force. It's less stimulation. That's rule one:
lower the noise, and your focus comes back. Your move today: protect the first thirty minutes.
Next, we take on the biggest myth about getting things done — that procrastination means you're lazy.
It doesn't, and Episode 2 shows you what it actually is. Subscribe so you don't miss it. New rules only."
**[ON-SCREEN:** End screen — Ep 2 "Procrastination Isn't Laziness" + Subscribe]** · Pinned comment:
"How long could you last without your phone in the morning? Be honest."
