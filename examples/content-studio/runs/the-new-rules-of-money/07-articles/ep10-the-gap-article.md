# How Do I Actually Build Wealth? The Gap Formula, Explained

**You build wealth from one number almost nobody names out loud: the *gap* — the space between what
you earn and what you spend — and what you do with it. Not your salary. Not your title. The gap.**
Most people attack one side of it: earn more, *or* spend less. Both work for a while, and both stall
alone. Earning has no ceiling, but raises quietly get eaten by nicer things. Saving has a floor —
you can't spend below zero — and it turns miserable long before you reach that floor. So the gap
stays frozen while you feel like you're trying hard.

The step almost everyone skips is the third one: you convert the gap into assets that produce
income. That income lands back on the *earn* side of your equation — money you didn't trade an hour
for — which widens the gap, which funds the next income-producing asset a little faster. Earn, keep
a gap, turn the gap into income, repeat. That's the whole formula. The first turn is painfully slow,
but a loop doesn't add — it compounds. Your job isn't to earn your way there or save your way there.
It's to widen the gap, protect it, and keep the loop turning. **The new rule: widen the gap, then
convert it to assets.**

---

## Why "earn more" and "spend less" both stall
"Earn more" sounds like the whole answer — until the raise actually lands. Then comes the nicer car,
the bigger place, the upgraded everything. You earn more, and the gap stays exactly where it was. A
bigger income with the same gap is just a more expensive way to stay broke.

So people flip to the other half-rule: "spend less." Cut the coffee, cancel the subscriptions,
white-knuckle every purchase. But cutting has a hard floor, and it's exhausting the whole way down.
Earning has no ceiling and saving has a floor, which is why neither side wins alone. The gap is the
thing — not the paycheck, not the penny-pinching, the *space* between them.

## The Gap → Asset Loop
Here's the part most people miss. A gap sitting in a checking account is just cash standing still. It
feels safe, but it's asleep. The move that changes everything is what we call the **Gap → Asset
Loop**: you take the gap and use it to acquire something that produces income. That income arrives on
the earn side of your equation without costing you an hour of work — which makes the gap wider, which
lets you acquire the next income-producing asset a little sooner.

Gap funds asset. Asset feeds gap. Gap funds the next asset. The first turn of the loop is slow and
unimpressive — a small asset, a trickle of income. But a loop doesn't add, it multiplies, and the
tenth turn looks nothing like the first. Cash sitting still is a gap asleep; an asset is a gap that
went to work.

## Lifestyle creep: the trap that feels like progress
Everything above assumes your gap actually grows when your income does. For most people, it doesn't —
and they never notice it happening. The culprit is **lifestyle creep**: every time your income rises,
your spending quietly rises to match it. New money in, new expenses out. Your paycheck grows, your
lifestyle grows, and the number that builds wealth stays parked at zero.

It's dangerous precisely because it feels like winning. You *are* earning more. You *do* have nicer
things. But the gap never moves, so the wealth never comes. Lifestyle creep is a raise that never
reaches the gap.

## How to widen your gap this week
1. **Name the gap.** Roughly, what's the space between what you earn and what you spend right now — a
   sliver, or growing? No exact numbers needed, just honesty.
2. **Intercept the next increase.** The next time your income ticks up — a raise, a bonus, a new
   client — decide the destination *before* the money arrives, so your lifestyle can't absorb it by
   default.
3. **Route it to the gap, not the lifestyle.** Let your spending rise slower than your income. That
   protected difference is the fuel the loop runs on.

## Frequently asked questions
**What exactly is "the gap"?**
The gap is the distance between what you earn and what you spend — the raw material of wealth. Two
people on identical salaries can have wildly different gaps, and the one with the wider gap (and a
plan for it) builds wealth while the other doesn't.

**Is it better to earn more or spend less?**
It's a false choice. Earning has no ceiling but tends to get eaten by lifestyle; saving has a floor
and gets miserable fast. You widen the gap from both sides, then convert it — that third step is what
actually compounds.

**Why doesn't my gap grow even when I get raises?**
Almost always lifestyle creep: spending rises in lockstep with income, so the gap never moves. The
fix is to decide where a raise goes before you feel it in your life.

**What's the single most important move?**
Catch your next bit of extra income before it disappears into your lifestyle, and send a fixed slice
of it straight to the gap. Do that once and you've already beaten the trap most people never see.
