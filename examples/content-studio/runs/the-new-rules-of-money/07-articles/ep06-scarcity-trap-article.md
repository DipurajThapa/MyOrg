# Why Do I Feel Broke Even When I Earn Enough? The Scarcity Trap

**You feel broke even when you earn enough because scarcity is a *mindset*, not a bank balance.
When your brain believes there isn't enough, it narrows — and that narrowing is what researchers
Sendhil Mullainathan and Eldar Shafir describe as a tax on your mental bandwidth. The worry about
money runs quietly in the background and eats the same attention you'd otherwise use to plan, resist
impulses, and think long-term. So the tight, anxious feeling isn't waiting on a bigger number. It's
waiting on rewired attention.** This is why a raise so often changes nothing. The scarcity wiring was
installed long before the money arrived: years of scanning for danger — *will the card decline, can
we cover rent* — taught your brain to treat "not enough" as the default, and that habit of attention
doesn't quit just because the threat is gone. The alarm keeps sounding in a room with no fire.

The fix isn't earning more. It's giving your brain proof that "enough" already exists — on purpose,
in advance — with a small, visible buffer that acts as a floor under you. **The new rule of the
scarcity trap: fix the scarcity wiring, not just the balance.**

---

## What is a scarcity mindset?
A scarcity mindset is the felt certainty that there's never quite enough — enough money, enough
time, enough room to breathe — regardless of what your account actually holds. It's not the same as
being poor. Someone with a comfortable income can carry it, and someone with very little can be
strangely free of it. What matters is the *belief*, because the belief is what your brain acts on.
Once "not enough" becomes the assumption, every decision gets filtered through it.

## Why a raise doesn't fix the feeling
Here's the counterintuitive part: more money often leaves the fear exactly where it was. If you
spent years bracing for the next shortfall, your brain got *good* at scarcity — it became a habit of
attention, a reflex. Habits don't switch off because the circumstances improved. Worse, a raise
tends to expand your spending and your worries at the same speed, so the gap between "enough" and
"safe" never actually closes. You didn't turn the alarm off. You just gave it a bigger house to
guard.

## The Bandwidth Tax
Think of your attention as a phone battery. Every unresolved money worry runs in the background — the
bill you haven't opened, the balance you're scared to check, the quiet what-if. You can't see those
apps running, but the battery still drains. Mullainathan and Shafir found that when people are
preoccupied by scarcity, their thinking takes a measurable hit — not because they're less capable,
but because their bandwidth is already spent. That's the trap: worse thinking leads to worse money
decisions, which creates more scarcity, which eats more bandwidth. Call it the **Bandwidth Tax** —
the most expensive tax nobody sends you a bill for.

## The moment scarcity actually robs you
The theft doesn't happen when you're broke. It happens the moment money finally shows up. A little
surplus lands, and the scarcity brain panics in reverse — spend it before it disappears, or freeze
and hoard it in fear. Either way, the surplus never gets *used* to build anything. If you've ever
received unexpected money and watched it evaporate within days, that reflex is the trap snapping
shut.

## How to rewire a scarcity mindset
You don't fix scarcity by getting rich. You fix it by giving your brain proof that "enough" already
exists — on purpose, in advance. The move: build one small, automatic buffer and name it *Safety*.
Not an investment. Not a goal. A number that sits there and does nothing but exist. Scarcity isn't
about the size of the pile; it's about the *feeling* of a floor under you. When a floor exists — even
a modest one — the background alarm quiets, your bandwidth returns, and you finally make the calm,
boring, long-term decisions scarcity was stealing from you.

## Frequently asked questions
**Is a scarcity mindset the same as actually being broke?**
No. Being broke is a number; a scarcity mindset is a lens. You can earn well and still see the world
through "not enough," and that lens is what shapes your decisions — often more than the balance does.

**Why does earning more sometimes make the anxiety worse?**
Because a raise usually expands your spending and your worries in lockstep, while the underlying
belief goes untouched. The number moves; the alarm doesn't. Fix the wiring, not just the balance.

**How does scarcity affect decisions I make with money?**
It narrows attention. The mental bandwidth you'd use to plan and resist impulses is already spent on
background worry, so you make more short-term, reactive choices — which tends to deepen the very
scarcity you're reacting to.

**What's the first step to feeling less broke?**
Install a floor. Open a separate space, automate one small amount into it, and label it *Safety* —
then watch what a visible floor does to your thinking. It's small, but it's proof.
