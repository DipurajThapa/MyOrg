**Smart people stay broke because of *money scripts* — invisible beliefs about money absorbed
early in life, usually before you could count. These scripts run a loop: your beliefs shape your
thoughts, your thoughts drive your emotions, your emotions drive your decisions, and your
decisions drive your bank balance. Intelligence barely enters into it.** You can know exactly what
you *should* do with money and still not do it, because behavior follows what you *believe*, not
what you *know*.

Here's the mechanism. People act to stay consistent with their self-image. If, deep down, you
believe you're "someone who's always broke," then when money starts to accumulate, you'll
unconsciously find a way to send it back — an emergency, a splurge, a bad deal — so reality matches
the story you hold about yourself. The most expensive money script of all is the sentence "I'm just
not good with money," because it quietly excuses you from ever trying.

The fix isn't more willpower or another budgeting app. It's editing the script. Catch the belief,
question it, and replace it — starting with one word: change "I'm *not* good with money" to "I'm
*learning* to be good with money." That single edit reopens the door. **The first new rule of money:
rewrite the story before you chase the money.**

---

## What is a money script?
A money script is a core belief about money you learned early — from family, culture, and
experience — and now run on autopilot. You didn't choose it; you absorbed it, like an accent.
Common ones: "money is stressful," "rich people are greedy," "there's never enough," "people like
us don't get wealthy." Each one silently steers your everyday decisions.

## Why intelligence doesn't protect you
Being broke has little to do with IQ. There are brilliant people deep in debt and ordinary people
quietly building wealth. If smarts were the deciding factor, every top student would be rich. The
real variable is **behavior**, and behavior is driven by belief, not knowledge. Knowing you should
save and actually saving are two different systems — and belief wins.

## The self-sabotage loop
When your self-image says "broke," money that arrives feels *inconsistent* with who you think you
are — and the mind hates inconsistency. So it corrects: an impulse buy, a "treat yourself," a loan
to a friend, a purchase you can't explain later. It feels like bad luck. It's actually the script
protecting your self-image.

## How to change a money mindset (3 steps)
1. **Spot** the script — catch the exact sentence you tell yourself about money.
2. **Question** it — is it true, or just inherited? Whose voice is it?
3. **Replace** it — swap in a truer, more useful belief, and act from it once.
Do this repeatedly. You're not faking positivity; you're editing a program that's been running
without your permission.

## Frequently asked questions
**Why am I always broke even though I earn well?**
Often it's a scarcity script plus lifestyle creep: as income rises, spending rises to match,
because the underlying belief ("there's never enough") never changed. Fix the wiring, not just the balance.

**Can you really change your money mindset?**
Yes — beliefs are learned, which means they're editable. It takes noticing the belief, questioning
it, and acting differently enough times that a new self-image forms.

**Is this the same as "manifesting" money?**
No. This isn't about wishing money into existence. It's about removing the self-sabotage that makes
smart, capable people undo their own progress.

**What's the first thing to do?**
Catch the sentence "I'm not good with money" and change one word — "not" to "learning." Small, but
it reopens a door the old script kept shut.
