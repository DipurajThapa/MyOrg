**VO:** "You're doing the math in your head right now. Your age, minus the years you feel like you

### [0:12–0:38] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here's the straight answer. No, it is not too late to build wealth — but not for the reason
motivational videos give. It's not too late because 'too late' was never about your age. It was about
which clock you were staring at. Most people read their **age** — the years behind them — and call it a
verdict. Wealth is built by reading your **runway** — the years still in front of you — and starting
from there. That's the new rule: the best time to build wealth is a mindset, not an age."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Age = years behind you. Runway = years ahead. You build from the runway.]**

### [0:38–0:56] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: why the 'you started too late' story is mostly a trick of memory, the
one honest limit that's real — because I won't lie to you about it — and a simple move that resets the
clock this week. Stay to the end, because the people who start late and still win almost always share
one thing, and it isn't money."

### [0:56–2:00] BODY 1 — The head-start myth
**VO:** "Let's kill the myth. The story goes: wealthy people got a head start — right school, right
decade, right timing — and if you didn't, the race was over before you laced up. It feels true. It's
mostly a trick of memory."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** record-scratch → "the race hasn't started"]**
**VO:** "Because you only count the years you 'lost.' You never count the years you still have. Someone
at fifty looks back at twenty missed years and grieves them — while ignoring the twenty, thirty,
sometimes forty years still ahead, which is more than enough time for money to do its slow, boring work.
You're mourning a head start while standing on a runway you refuse to look at."
**[ON-SCREEN:** You count the years you lost. Wealth counts the years you have left.]**
> You're not late to the race. You've been staring at the wrong end of the track.

### [2:00–3:05] BODY 2 — The Second Clock
**VO:** "So here's the original idea — call it the **Second Clock**. You've got two clocks running. The
First Clock counts your age. It only goes up, it only reminds you of what's gone, and it's the one broke
thinking checks obsessively. The Second Clock counts your runway — the years between right now and the
day you'd want to stop working. And here's what matters: you get to *start* the Second Clock. It begins
the moment you decide to read it instead of the first one."
**[VISUAL:** two clocks — one labeled AGE ticking up in grey, one labeled RUNWAY starting fresh in cream]**
**VO:** "A twenty-five-year-old and a fifty-year-old can have the exact same Second Clock — the same
runway to a goal. The difference isn't the time they have. It's which clock runs their decisions. One is
planning from the years ahead. The other is apologizing for the years behind."
> The First Clock tells you how long you've lived. The Second tells you how long you've got. Only one builds wealth.

### [3:05–3:35] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "Now, I promised honesty, so here's what the feel-good videos skip. Starting later does cost you
something real — it costs you *slack*. A twenty-year-old can be sloppy, quit for a year, make a dumb bet,
and time forgives it. Start at fifty and you have less room for error — which means your edge can't be
time anymore. It has to be consistency. And that's good news, because consistency isn't sold to the
young. It's built by the people who finally got serious."

### [3:35–4:40] BODY 3 — What the late starters have in common
**VO:** "Which brings us to what they share — the people who start late and still build real wealth. It
isn't a windfall. It isn't a secret asset. It's that they stopped negotiating with the past and made the
runway the only number that counts. They think in gaps, not ages: earn a little more than you spend, and
send that gap somewhere it can grow. That formula doesn't check your birthday before it works."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Wealth is the gap between what you earn and what you spend — at any age.]**
**VO:** "Take the housing story people your age love to bring up. A first-time buyer in 1990 could save
a down payment in around three years. Today, in many places, that same climb takes closer to a decade.
It's easy to hear that and conclude the door shut. But notice — that number is about *how long*, not
*whether*. The person who starts the ten-year climb today finishes it. The person who decides it's
hopeless never starts, and guarantees the outcome they feared."
> The old rules were built for an easier decade. That's not a reason to quit — it's a reason to start with your eyes open.

### [4:40–5:05] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — is it too late? No. 'Too late' was never a number. It was the First Clock convincing you the
game ended because *you* felt behind. Your one move this week: start the Second Clock. Write down the age
you'd like to stop needing a paycheck. Subtract today. That number — the years ahead, not behind — is your
real starting line. Plan from that, and only that. Next, we pull the whole series together: every old rule
retired, every new rule, in one plan you can run in 2026. That's Episode 15, the capstone. Subscribe so
the last rule lands. New rules only."
