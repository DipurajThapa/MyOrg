# Ep 6 Script — "The Scarcity Trap: Why Feeling Broke Keeps You Broke"

**OWNS THE QUESTION:** Why do I feel broke even when I earn enough?
**KEYWORD:** scarcity mindset money · **OLD RULE:** "More money ends the fear." →
**NEW RULE:** "Fix the scarcity wiring, not just the balance."

---

### [0:00–0:12] COLD-OPEN HOOK
**VO:** "You got the raise. The number in your account went up. And the tight, anxious feeling in
your chest? Still there. If earning more never made you feel less broke, you're not ungrateful.
You're running software a bigger paycheck can't patch."
**[ON-SCREEN:** THE SCARCITY TRAP]** · [a bank balance rising while a chest-tightness ring stays red]

### [0:12–0:35] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here's the short answer. You feel broke even when you earn enough because scarcity is a
*mindset*, not a bank balance. When your brain believes there's not enough, it narrows — and that
narrowing is what researchers Sendhil Mullainathan and Eldar Shafir call a tax on your mental
bandwidth. Feeling short on money quietly eats the same attention you'd use to plan, resist impulses,
and think long-term. So the fear isn't waiting on a bigger number. It's waiting on rewired attention.
That's the new rule: **fix the scarcity wiring, not just the balance.**"
**[ON-SCREEN:** Scarcity is a mindset, not a balance → it taxes your bandwidth]**

### [0:35–0:52] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: what scarcity actually does to your brain, why a raise so often
makes it *worse*, and the one move that starts to switch it off. Stay to the end — because there's a
specific moment scarcity hijacks your decisions, and once you can see it, you can't be robbed by it
again."

### [0:52–1:55] BODY 1 — Scarcity is a tax, not a mood
**VO:** "Scarcity isn't just a feeling. It's a load. Picture your attention as a phone battery. Every
worry about money runs in the background — the unpaid bill, the number you're scared to check, the
what-if. You can't see the apps running, but the battery still drains."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** phone-battery icon dropping from 100% to 12% — "background apps: MONEY"]**
**VO:** "Mullainathan and Shafir found something striking: when people are preoccupied by scarcity,
their thinking takes a hit as real as losing a full night of sleep. Not because they're less
capable — because their bandwidth is already spent. And here's the trap: worse thinking leads to
worse money decisions, which creates more scarcity, which eats more bandwidth. I call it **the
Bandwidth Tax** — the most expensive tax nobody sends you a bill for."
**[ON-SCREEN:** The Bandwidth Tax — scarcity spends the attention you need to escape it]**
> Being broke doesn't just cost you money. It quietly taxes the very attention you'd use to fix it.

### [1:55–2:55] BODY 2 — Why the raise doesn't fix it
**VO:** "So why doesn't more money end the fear? Because the wiring was installed long before the
raise. If you spent years scanning for danger — will the card decline, can we cover rent — your brain
got *good* at scarcity. It became a habit of attention. And habits don't quit just because the threat
is gone."
**[VISUAL:** a smoke alarm still shrieking in an empty, calm kitchen]**
**VO:** "That's why people who objectively earn enough still feel broke. The alarm keeps sounding in a
room with no fire. Worse — a raise often expands your spending and your worries at the same speed, so
the gap between 'enough' and 'safe' never closes. You didn't turn the alarm off. You just gave it a
bigger house to guard."
**[ON-SCREEN:** A raise moves the number. It doesn't move the alarm.]**
> You can out-earn your income. You can't out-earn a nervous system that's sure there's never enough.

### [2:55–3:20] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "Now watch closely — here's the moment I promised, the exact point scarcity robs you. It's not
when you're broke. It's when money finally shows up. A little surplus lands, and the scarcity brain
panics in reverse: spend it before it disappears, or freeze and hoard it in fear. Either way, the
surplus never gets *used* to build anything. That reflex is where the trap snaps shut. So let's pry
it open."

### [3:20–4:30] BODY 3 — The one move that rewires it
**VO:** "You don't fix scarcity by getting rich. You fix it by giving your brain proof that 'enough'
already exists — on purpose, in advance. The move: build one small, automatic buffer, and name it
*Safety*. Not an investment. Not a goal. A number that sits there and does nothing but exist. Because
scarcity isn't about the size of the pile — it's about the *feeling* of a floor under you."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** a thin cream line slides under a falling figure — "FLOOR INSTALLED"]**
**VO:** "When there's a floor — even a small one — the background alarm gets quieter. Your bandwidth
comes back. And with your attention returned, you finally make the calm, boring, long-term decisions
scarcity was stealing from you. That's the switch. Not more money. A visible floor, and proof that
this time, the surplus gets to stay."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Scarcity ends the day your brain believes there's a floor — not the day you get rich.]**
> A buffer isn't just money in an account. It's a message to your brain: the emergency is over.

### [4:30–5:05] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — you don't feel broke because you *are* broke. You feel broke because scarcity taxed your
bandwidth and left the alarm running. And that alarm answers to a floor, not a raise. That's the new
rule: fix the scarcity wiring, not just the balance. Your one move this week: open a separate space,
automate one small amount into it, and label it *Safety*. Watch what a floor does to your thinking.
Next episode, we go after scarcity's twin — that knot of guilt you feel even when you *can* afford it.
Episode 7 shows you where money guilt comes from and how to shut it off. Subscribe so you don't miss
it. New rules only."
