**OWNS THE QUESTION:** Why can't I stop overspending?
**KEYWORD:** why can't I stop overspending psychology · **OLD RULE:** "You overspend because you

### [0:00–0:12] COLD-OPEN HOOK
**VO:** "You know exactly how much is in your account. You know you shouldn't. You buy it anyway —
and for about ten minutes, you feel better. If that's you, stop calling it a discipline problem.
It was never about the money. It was about the feeling you had right before you paid."
**[ON-SCREEN:** WHY YOU CAN'T STOP OVERSPENDING]** · [a shopping cart with a small storm cloud over it]

### [0:12–0:35] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "So — why can't you stop overspending? Because overspending isn't a math problem. It's an
emotional one. You're not buying the thing; you're buying relief. Stress, boredom, loneliness,
feeling small — an uncomfortable emotion shows up, and spending is the fastest way you've found to
make it go quiet. That's why more willpower never works: you're aiming discipline at a wound. The fix
isn't a stricter budget. It's a single skill — **name the emotion you're soothing.** That's the new
rule."
**[ON-SCREEN:** You're not buying the thing. You're buying relief.]**

### [0:35–0:52] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: why this is emotional and not mathematical, the exact loop that
fires every time you overspend, and the one pause that breaks it. Stay to the end — because there's a
two-word question that stops an impulse buy cold, and almost nobody asks it."

### [0:52–1:55] BODY 1 — It's emotional, not mathematical
**VO:** "Let's kill the discipline myth. If overspending were about math, a spreadsheet would fix it.
It doesn't. People with high incomes and full awareness still do it — because the trigger isn't a
number, it's a feeling. Notice when you overspend: rarely when you're calm. Almost always when you're
stressed, bored, drained, or celebrating a little too hard."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** record-scratch → "it's not a spreadsheet problem"]**
**VO:** "Spending works because it delivers a tiny, reliable hit of *something happening*. For a
moment you're in control, you're rewarded, the discomfort lifts. Your brain files it away: bad
feeling in, purchase out, relief. It's not weakness. It's a coping tool that runs on your money."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Overspending is a coping tool that runs on money.]**
> You don't have a spending problem. You have a soothing habit that costs money.

### [1:55–3:00] BODY 2 — The trigger → spend loop
**VO:** "Here's the loop, and once you see it you can't unsee it. **Trigger. Urge. Spend. Relief —
then regret.** A trigger hits: a hard day, a fight, a scroll through everyone else's highlight reel.
That builds an urge — restless, itchy, *I just need something*. You spend. Relief floods in, real and
immediate. Then, minutes later, the regret — which becomes a new bad feeling, which becomes the next
trigger."
**[VISUAL:** a four-stage ring — trigger → urge → spend → relief/regret — spinning faster]**
**VO:** "Notice the trap. The regret at the end doesn't stop the loop. It fuels it. That's why 'just
try harder' fails — you're trying to willpower your way out of a wheel that spins on its own."
> The regret doesn't break the loop. It becomes the next trigger.

### [3:00–3:25] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "So if you can't out-discipline the loop, how do you break it? You don't attack the spend.
You attack the gap between the urge and the swipe — because right now there isn't one. Urge and
purchase happen in the same breath. Put one small pause in that gap, and the whole wheel loses its
grip. Here's the pause."

### [3:25–4:35] BODY 3 — The pause that breaks it  (open-loop payoff)
**VO:** "The loop runs on speed. It needs you to act before you think. So you slow it down on purpose.
The moment you feel the urge — hand hovering over 'buy now' — you pause, and you ask two words:
**'Feeling what?'** That's it. Not 'can I afford this.' Not 'do I need this.' Those are math
questions, and this was never math."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** freeze-frame on the cursor over "buy now" → "name it first"]**
**VO:** "And the second you name it — 'I'm stressed,' 'I'm lonely,' 'I feel behind' — something
shifts. Naming a feeling turns down its volume; it moves the moment from your reflexes to your
thinking brain. Suddenly you're not a person who *has* to buy. You're a person having a feeling, who
noticed. Half the time the urge just deflates, because it was never about the thing in the cart. Buy
it or don't — but now *you're* choosing, not the loop."
**[ON-SCREEN:** The pause: "Feeling what?"]**
> Name the feeling and it loses its grip. You can't spend your way out of an emotion you won't name.

### [4:35–5:10] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — you don't overspend because you're weak. You overspend because it works, for about ten
minutes, as a way to not feel something. That's the new rule: you're soothing an emotion — so name
it. Your move this week: next time your hand's over the button, pause and ask two words — 'Feeling
what?' — and say the answer out loud. That's the whole practice. Next episode, we go one level
deeper — into the feeling *underneath* so many of these urges: the sense of not-enough that follows
you even when the numbers say you're fine. Episode 6 is the scarcity trap. Subscribe so you don't
miss it. New rules only."
