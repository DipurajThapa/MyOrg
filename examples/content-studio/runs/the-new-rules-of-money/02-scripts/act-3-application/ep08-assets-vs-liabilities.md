**OWNS THE QUESTION:** What are assets vs liabilities, really?
**KEYWORD:** assets vs liabilities explained · **OLD RULE:** "Your house and your car are assets." →

### [0:00–0:12] COLD-OPEN HOOK
**VO:** "Most of what you call your 'assets' are quietly making you poorer. Your car. Maybe your house.
That gadget you financed. On paper they look like wealth. In real life, every month, they reach into

### [0:12–0:35] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here's the short version. An asset and a liability aren't defined by what a thing *is* —
they're defined by which way the money moves. An **asset** puts money *into* your pocket. A
**liability** takes money *out*. That's the whole rule. Forget the accounting definition you
half-remember from school. Ask one question about anything you own: does it pay me, or does it cost
me? That's the new rule of money: **an asset pays you; a liability costs you.**"
**[ON-SCREEN:** Asset → money IN · Liability → money OUT]**

### [0:35–0:52] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: why the schoolbook definition quietly bankrupts smart people, the
one test that sorts anything in five seconds, and how to re-label the things you own so they stop
lying to you. Stay to the end — because the item most people are *proudest* of usually turns out to
be their biggest liability, and you probably parked it in the driveway."

### [0:52–1:55] BODY 1 — The schoolbook definition is wrong
**VO:** "Let's kill the definition you were handed. In school, an asset is anything you own that has
value. By that logic your car, your couch, the shoes in your closet — all assets. Technically true.
Practically useless. Because 'things I own' and 'things that make me wealthier' are two completely
different lists — and confusing them is why people who *own* a lot still *have* nothing."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** record-scratch → "owning ≠ wealth"]**
**VO:** "The old definition measures what you *have*. The new one measures what your stuff *does*."
**[ON-SCREEN:** The old definition counts what you own. The new one counts what pays you.]**
> Owning things isn't wealth. Owning things that pay you is.

### [1:55–3:00] BODY 2 — The cash-flow test (the Arrow Test)
**VO:** "So here's the tool. I call it the **Arrow Test**, and it works on anything. Picture an arrow
between you and the thing you own. Every month, which way does it point? If money flows *from the
thing to you*, it's an asset. If money flows *from you to the thing* — insurance, fuel, interest,
upkeep, the subscription — it's a liability. No spreadsheet. Just the arrow."
**[VISUAL:** a person; an arrow flipping between "→ you" (green) and "you →" (red)]**
**VO:** "Run it on a car. It burns fuel, needs insurance, loses value the second you drive off the
lot, and charges interest if you financed it. Four arrows, all pointing *away*. A car is one of the
most expensive liabilities most people will ever own. It's not evil — it's just honest to call it
what it is, so you stop mistaking a bill for a blessing."
> A car doesn't pay you. It bills you — in fuel, interest, and quiet depreciation.

### [3:00–3:25] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "Now, before the comments explode — 'but my house!' Here's the uncomfortable part. Your home
can be either one, and the Arrow Test doesn't care about your feelings. If it only ever takes —
mortgage interest, taxes, repairs, the boiler that dies in January — the arrow points away, and right
now it behaves like a liability. That's not an insult. It's the truth that lets you plan around it
instead of pretending."

### [3:25–4:30] BODY 3 — Re-sorting your 'assets'  (open-loop payoff)
**VO:** "Take everything you'd call an asset and drop each one through the Arrow Test. The financed
car? Liability. The wardrobe you 'invested' in? Liability. The phone on a 24-month plan? Liability.
Now the other column — the things that point the arrow back *at* you. A skill that earns. Something
you own that others pay to use. Money set aside that quietly works instead of sitting still. You don't
need many. You need the arrows pointing the right way."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Sort every 'asset': does the arrow point AT you, or AWAY?]**
**VO:** "So here's your one job. Don't buy anything. Just re-label. Make two columns — 'pays me' and
'costs me' — and put every big thing you own in the honest column. When you do, the reason the money
keeps vanishing stops being a mystery. It's all sitting in the wrong column."
> Wealth isn't the size of your pile. It's how many arrows point back at you.

### [4:30–5:05] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — an asset isn't what you own. It's what pays you. A liability isn't a scary word. It's
anything the arrow points away from. That's the new rule: an asset pays you; a liability costs you.
Your move today: sort your two columns, honestly, no flinching. Once you can see the arrows, the next
question writes itself — how do you keep more of what you earn without tracking every penny? Because
budgeting, the way you were taught it, almost never works. Episode 9 shows you what to do instead.
Subscribe so you don't miss it. New rules only."
