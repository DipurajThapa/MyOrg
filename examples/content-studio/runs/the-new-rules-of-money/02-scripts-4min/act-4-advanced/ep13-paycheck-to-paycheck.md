### [0:12–0:38] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here's the short version. You stop living paycheck to paycheck by building a small buffer —
one week of expenses sitting between you and your bills — then automating the gap so saving happens
before you can spend it. Not by earning more. A buffer breaks the cycle because your next paycheck is
no longer spoken for the second it lands. That's the new rule of money: **you don't out-earn the
paycheck cycle — you out-structure it, at any income.**"
**[ON-SCREEN:** BUFFER (one week) + AUTOMATE the gap = out of the cycle]**

### [0:38–0:52] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: why more income never fixes this, the one buffer that breaks the
cycle, and how to automate it. Stay to the end — there's a specific reason your last raise vanished,
and until you see it, every raise after it vanishes the same way."

### [0:52–1:45] BODY 1 — Why more income doesn't fix it
**VO:** "Let's kill the myth: 'I just need to earn more.' It feels obviously true — and it's why people
stay stuck at every income level. When the raise lands, your spending quietly rises to meet it.
Economists call it lifestyle inflation: spending expands to match income, almost automatically."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** record-scratch → "the raise didn't disappear — you absorbed it"]**
**VO:** "So the gap between what you make and what you spend stays right where it was — near zero — just
with bigger numbers on both sides. That's why the doctor and the barista can both be one missed check
from panic. This was never about the size of the paycheck. It's the distance between it and your bills
— and right now, that distance is zero."
**[ON-SCREEN:** More income, same gap. The distance is the problem — not the paycheck.]**
> A raise doesn't shrink the cycle. It just re-sizes the same trap.

### [1:45–2:05] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "Here's the trap even people who know all this fall into. They decide to build the buffer 'with
whatever's left at the end of the month.' And every month, there's nothing left — because 'whatever's
left' is a lie you tell yourself while you spend it. Willpower loses this fight every time. So the fix
can't depend on you remembering, or resisting."

### [2:05–3:35] BODY 2 — The Week Wall + automate the gap  (open-loop payoff)
**VO:** "So here's the fix, and it starts smaller than you think. Not three months of savings — that
number's so big people quit before they start. Call it **the Week Wall**: one single week of your
expenses, parked in a separate account, never touched unless everything else runs out. Why one week?
Because one week is the difference between reacting and choosing. Right now, if a bill lands two days
early, you scramble — that scramble is the cycle. The Week Wall absorbs the timing. Your bills hit the
wall, not you."
**[VISUAL:** a small brick wall slides between an incoming paycheck and a stack of bills]**
> One week of buffer is the shortest distance between panic and a plan.
**VO:** "And here's what makes it stick: **pay the wall first, automatically.** The day money comes in,
a small fixed amount moves to your buffer before you ever see it — the first slice, not the leftovers.
Automate it once and it happens whether you're motivated or not. That's why it works when budgeting
doesn't: budgeting asks you to resist temptation forty times a month; automation asks you to decide
once. Your last raise vanished because it landed in a spendable account with no wall in front of it.
Put the wall up, automate the slice, and the next raise has somewhere to go besides your lifestyle."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Automate the gap: money leaves before you can spend it.]**
> You don't need more discipline. You need one automatic transfer and a wall it can't cross.

### [3:35–4:00] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — you don't escape the paycheck cycle by earning more. You escape it by putting one week of
expenses between you and your bills, and automating the slice that builds it. Your one move this week:
open a separate account, name it the Week Wall, set one small automatic transfer into it today. Once
the wall's up and the panic's gone, the real question is: is it too late to build wealth from here? If
part of you thinks you started too late, Episode 14 is for you. Subscribe so you don't miss it. New
rules only."
