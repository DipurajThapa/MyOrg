### [0:12–0:40] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "Here's the straight answer. No, it is not too late to build wealth. 'Too late' was never about
your age — it was about which clock you were staring at. Most people read their **age** — the years
behind them — and call it a verdict. Wealth is
built by reading your **runway** — the years still in front of you — and starting from there. That's
the new rule: the best time to build wealth is a mindset, not an age."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Age = years behind you. Runway = years ahead. You build from the runway.]**

### [0:40–0:54] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes: why the 'you started too late' story is mostly a trick of memory, the
one honest limit that's real, and a simple move that resets the clock this week. Stay to the end —
late starters who still win almost always share one thing, and it isn't money."

### [0:54–2:25] BODY 1 — The head-start myth + the Second Clock
**VO:** "Let's kill the myth: the wealthy got a head start — right school, right decade — and if you
didn't, the race was over before you started. It feels true. It's mostly a trick of memory: you count
the years you 'lost,' never the years you still have."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** record-scratch → "the race hasn't started"]**
**VO:** "Someone at fifty grieves twenty missed years while ignoring the thirty or forty still ahead —
more than enough time for money to do its slow, boring work."
> You're not late to the race. You've been staring at the wrong end of the track.
**VO:** "So here's the original idea — the **Second Clock**. You've got two clocks running. The First
Clock counts your age: it only goes up, reminds you what's gone, the clock broke thinking checks
obsessively. The Second Clock counts your runway — the years between now and the day you'd want to stop
working. And you get to *start* it, the moment you read it instead of the first one. A twenty-five-year-old
and a fifty-year-old can have the exact same Second Clock. The difference isn't the time they have —
it's which clock runs their decisions."
**[VISUAL:** two clocks — one labeled AGE ticking up in grey, one labeled RUNWAY starting fresh in cream]**
> The First Clock tells you how long you've lived. The Second tells you how long you've got. Only one builds wealth.

### [2:25–2:50] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "I promised honesty — here's what feel-good videos skip. Starting later does cost you
something real — *slack*. A twenty-year-old can be sloppy, quit for a year, and time forgives it. Start
at fifty and you have less room for error — so your edge can't be time. It has to be consistency. And
that's good news: consistency isn't sold to the young. It's built by people who finally got serious."

### [2:50–3:40] BODY 2 — What the late starters have in common
**VO:** "Which brings us to what they share. It isn't a windfall or a secret asset. It's that they
stopped negotiating with the past and made the runway the only number that counts. They think in gaps,
not ages: earn a little more than you spend, and send that gap somewhere it can grow. That formula
doesn't check your birthday. Take the housing story. A first-time buyer in 1990 could
save a down payment in around three years; today, in many places, that climb takes closer to a decade.
Easy to hear that and decide the door shut. But notice — that's about *how long*, not *whether*. The
person who starts the ten-year climb today finishes it. The one who calls it hopeless never starts, and
guarantees the outcome they feared."
**[ON-SCREEN:** Wealth is the gap between what you earn and what you spend — at any age.]**
> The old rules were built for an easier decade. That's not a reason to quit — it's a reason to start with your eyes open.

### [3:40–4:00] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — is it too late? No. 'Too late' was never a number. It was the First Clock convincing you
