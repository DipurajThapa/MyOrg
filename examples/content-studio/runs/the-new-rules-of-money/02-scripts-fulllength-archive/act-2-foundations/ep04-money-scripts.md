# Ep 4 Script — "The Money Scripts You Inherited (and How to Rewrite Them)"

**OWNS THE QUESTION:** What is a money mindset and how do I change it?
**KEYWORD:** money mindset how to change · **OLD RULE:** "Money beliefs are fixed — you either have

### [0:00–0:12] COLD-OPEN HOOK
**VO:** "You didn't choose how you feel about money. You inherited it. Before you could add two
numbers, you already had rules about what money means, who gets to have it, and what kind of person
you are. Nobody handed you those rules on paper. But you've been obeying them your whole life."
**[ON-SCREEN:** THE MONEY SCRIPTS YOU INHERITED]** · [an old handwritten note being passed hand to hand]

### [0:12–0:38] GEO ANSWER BLOCK  ← citation target
**VO:** "So — what is a money mindset, and how do you change it? A money mindset is the set of
inherited beliefs you run about money — a *money script*. That script runs a chain: a belief
becomes a thought, the thought becomes an emotion, the emotion becomes an action, and the action
becomes your result. You can't change the result by force. You change it by editing the script at
the top. And a script is just words — which means it can be rewritten. That's the new rule:
**your money story is editable.**"
**[ON-SCREEN:** Belief → Thought → Emotion → Action → Result]**

### [0:38–0:58] VALUE PROMISE + OPEN LOOP
**VO:** "In the next few minutes I'll show you where your script was written, why it feels like
plain fact instead of an opinion, and a simple three-step method to rewrite it — Spot, Question,
Replace. Stay to the end, because there's one line almost everyone inherited, and it's quietly
running your bank account right now."

### [0:58–2:10] BODY 1 — A script isn't a fact
**VO:** "First, let's clear up the biggest confusion. A money belief doesn't *feel* like a belief.
It feels like the truth. When you think 'talking about money is rude' or 'wanting more is greedy,'
your brain doesn't file that as an opinion you picked up. It files it as reality. That's what makes
it powerful — you defend it without ever examining it."
**[PATTERN INTERRUPT:** record-scratch → "that's not a fact — it's a script"]**
**VO:** "But here's the tell. Hand the same situation to two people with two different scripts and
they'll see two different worlds. One sees an opportunity; one sees a threat. Same facts. Different
script. Which means the script was never the truth — it was just yours."
**[ON-SCREEN:** A money belief feels like a fact. It's a script.]**
> A money belief doesn't feel like a belief. It feels like the truth. That's the trap.

### [2:10–3:20] BODY 2 — Where the script was written
**VO:** "So where did yours come from? Mostly, from a childhood you barely remember. You watched how
the adults around you handled money — the sighs at the mailbox, the arguments behind a closed door,
the silence when you asked how much something cost. You weren't taught money. You were marinated in
it. And a child's job is to make sense of the room, so you wrote rules to survive it: 'Money is
stress.' 'Wanting things causes fights.' 'People like us don't get to relax about this.'"
**[VISUAL:** a kid at a kitchen table, a stack of bills, a parent's tired face]**
**VO:** "Those rules made sense when you were seven. The problem is you're not seven anymore — and
you're still running seven-year-old software on a grown-up salary. The script outlived the room it
was written for."
> You're running seven-year-old money software on a grown-up salary.

### [3:20–3:55] MID-ROLL RE-HOOK
**VO:** "Now here's the part that changes everything. You can't delete a script. Willpower doesn't
reach it — you can't out-discipline a belief you don't even know you hold. But you *can* edit it.
And editing has three moves. This is the method — I call it **Spot, Question, Replace.**"

### [3:55–5:05] BODY 3 — Spot → Question → Replace  (open-loop payoff)
**VO:** "**Step one — Spot.** Catch the script in the wild. It usually shows up as a sentence in
your head right before a money decision: 'I could never afford that.' 'That's not for people like
me.' Name it out loud. You can't edit what you can't see.
**Step two — Question.** Ask it one thing: *is this actually true, or is it just old?* Whose voice
is this — mine, or someone else's from a long time ago? Beliefs shrink the second you make them
prove themselves.
**Step three — Replace.** Write the new line. Not a fantasy — a truer, more useful one. 'I could
never afford that' becomes 'I haven't prioritized that yet.' See the shift? One version closes the
door and calls it fate. The other keeps the door open and hands you the keys."
**[ON-SCREEN:** SPOT → QUESTION → REPLACE]**
**VO:** "And here's the line almost everyone inherited — the one I promised you: **'That's just how
I am with money.'** That sentence isn't a description. It's a lock. Run it through the three steps.
Spot it. Question it — is it true, or just old? Replace it: '**That's how I *used* to be with
money.**' Past tense. Because the moment your story becomes something you *had*, it stops being
something you *are*."
> 'That's just how I am with money' is not a description. It's a lock — and you hold the key.

### [5:05–5:40] CLOSE — PAYOFF + CTA + NEXT EPISODE
**VO:** "So — your money mindset isn't your personality. It's a script, written by a room you've
long since left, and scripts get rewritten. That's the new rule: your money story is editable. Your
move this week: catch one inherited line, and run it through Spot, Question, Replace. Just one.
Next episode, we take the script into the real world — because there's one place these old beliefs
hijack you every single time: the moment you overspend. Episode 5 shows you why you can't stop, and
how to break it. Subscribe so you don't miss it. New rules only."
